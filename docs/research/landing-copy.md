**Title:** What if space could be read through experience?

**Introduction:** Predictive Atmospheres asks how the physical character of a room relates to what people report feeling and to signals recorded from the body. The project links architectural attributes, forehead EEG, wrist ECG and self-report in a pilot study of rendered rooms, then tests whether those relationships can inform a design tool.

## Why this question

Architects can describe geometry, light and intended use with precision, but an occupant's changing experience is harder to represent in the same design workflow. The thesis calls this gap the *static environment problem*. Predictive Atmospheres investigates a way to place human response beside spatial description, while keeping measured signals, reported feelings and constructed interpretations distinct.

## Research question and hypothesis

**Question:** Can linked human and spatial metrics help describe experiences of architectural rooms and support an experimental design feedback loop?

**Working hypothesis:** If room form, lighting and function are recorded alongside quality-checked physiology and self-report, their relationships may reveal patterns that a spatial model can test. A fused valence–arousal position is one proposed representation; its usefulness must be evaluated against separate components and simple baselines.

## Three experiments, one linked method

**Form.** Room geometry varies while viewers report comfort. Comfort remains its own measure.

**Lighting.** Room openings and supplied luminous attributes vary. Reported valence and arousal are considered alongside physiological features, with the distinction between source metadata and calibrated headset exposure made explicit.

**Function.** Rendered space types vary. Ratings and body-derived features can be compared across the repeated experiences of the pilot participants.

Across the experiments, source room attributes and original renders are linked by room and trial IDs to EEG, ECG and available reports. Eligibility is checked before comparison. The atlas separates physiology-derived, self-reported and fused coordinates so readers can inspect both agreement and disagreement.

## Contribution and invitation

The work contributes a linked room–response dataset, a documented analysis path and an experimental Design Studio. The studio scores a proposed room's predicted fused position against a visitor's chosen emotional target and can search for a feasible configuration close to a requested numerical Neuro-Score. Its schematic geometry and studied-room references are identified as such; predictions are limited by the evaluated model.
